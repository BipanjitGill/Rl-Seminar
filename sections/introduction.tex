\section{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

% What the paper is about. Motivation for the problem at hand. Always in your own words, your understanding.

This research paper presents \textbf{Transformer Programs}, a new approach to tackle the challenge of understanding how Transformer models work. Despite the exceptional performance of transformer models across various domains, these are often perceived as complex "black boxes," as it is difficult to interpret their decision-making processes. This lack of transparency raises concerns about their reliability, particularly in critical applications where understanding the model's reasoning is essential.

The main motivation for this work is the limitation of existing interpretability methods. Techniques like analyzing attention patterns or probing hidden representations provide partial insights and fail to uncover the underlying algorithms. Efforts to reverse-engineer Transformers into interpretable components requires significant manual effort and are often hindered by model complexity. Similarly, converting human-written programs into Transformer networks does not clarify the workings of a standard transformer.

This research addresses these challenges by introducing some constraints to the standard Transformer model, training it, and converting it into human-readable programs that implement the same algorithm. This approach enables the development of models that are interpretable by design, offering a clearer understanding of their functionality.
