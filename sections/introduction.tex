\section{Introduction}
\label{ch:introduction}

% What the paper is about. Motivation for the problem at hand. Always in your own words, your understanding.

This research paper presents Transformer Programs, a new approach to tackle the challenge of understanding how Transformer models work. While these models are known for their outstanding performance in many areas, they are often seen as complex "black boxes", making it hard to explain how they make decisions. This lack of transparency raises concerns about their trustworthiness, especially in important applications where it's crucial to understand their reasoning.

The main motivation for this work is the limitation of existing interpretability methods. While techniques like analyzing attention patterns and probing hidden representations offer some insights, they often provide incomplete or potentially misleading explanations and fall short of revealing the underlying algorithms. Some researchers also try to reverse-engineer Transformers into understandable components but it requires significant manual effort and can be hindered by model complexity. Some try to convert human-written programs into Transformer networks but it does not give information about how a standard model works.

This research tries to tackle these issues by adding some constraints on the standard transformer model, train it and then converted it into human-readable programs that implement same algorithm. This approach enables the creation of models that are interpretable by design, providing a clearer understanding of how they function.
