The authors validate their approach by conducting experiments on various tasks, demonstrating the effectiveness and interpretability of Transformer Programs.

\subsection{In-Context Learning}
\label{subsec:incontext-results-analysis}
The authors initially demonstrate the capabilities of Transformer Programs on a simple in-context learning task by \textcite{brown2020language}, in which the model takes a sequence of alternating letters and numbers ending with a letter. If the letter has appeared before, it outputs the corresponding number; if not, it outputs an "unk" token. Model contain only two layers, one attention head per layer. The final embedding include four variables: tokens, positions, and one output variable for each attention head. The model achieves perfect accuracy on test set. The residual streams and attention heads are illustrated in \cref{fig:incontext} and \cref{fig:incontext-attention}. 
The first attention head focuses on the position for both key and query. At each position, it looks at the key from the previous step and writes the token's value. The second attention head uses tokens as the query and the output from the first head as the key. If the query is a letter, it tries to match it with a key in the first head's output that has the same letter followed by a number.

\subsection{RASP Tasks}

These are algorithmic tasks were introduced by \textcite{weiss2021thinking}. On five out of seven tasks, the learned programs achieve over 99\% accuracy as shown in Table \cref{tab:rasp-task-results}, demonstrating their effectiveness in handling diverse algorithmic problems. In general, standard Transformers outperform Transformer Programs, particularly on tasks with longer sequences and larger vocabularies as shown in the \cref{fig:comp-with-std}. Both models show a similar trend: their performance declines as task complexity increases. Notably, Transformer Programs experience a sharper performance drop compared to standard transformer. This suggests that while Transformer Programs show promise, further optimization is needed to match the performance of standard Transformers on more challenging tasks.

\subsection{NLP Tasks: Named Entity Recognition and Text Classification}

In this dataset, each word is labelled as four entity type  (PER, ORG, LOC, MISC) or not a entity (O). The transformer is trained with only categorical attention heads. Overall, the results for both NER and text classification tasks demonstrate the capability of Transformer Programs to learn effective and interpretable solutions for complex natural language processing problems. The comparable performance to standard Transformers, combined with the ability to understand the programs' decision-making logic, makes this approach a promising direction for building more transparent and trustworthy AI systems.
